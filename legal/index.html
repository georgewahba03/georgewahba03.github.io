Overview

I am beginning a JD in 2026 after five years of computer engineering at McMaster University, including co-op terms in embedded software and FPGA design. This page collects the legal and legal-adjacent side of my work.

The two tracks are not separate exercises. Much of the law that matters most right now — privacy, product liability, procurement, intellectual property, algorithmic accountability — turns on technical questions that are difficult to argue about without having built the thing. I intend to be useful precisely at that seam.

Areas of Interest

Intellectual property and patent practice. An engineering degree is a prerequisite for patent agent qualification in Canada, and hardware and embedded systems are the subject matter I can read fastest.

Access to justice and legal technology. Court and tribunal data in Canada is public in principle and difficult to use in practice. Tooling that closes that gap is where my software background does the most good.

Corporate law. Mergers and acquisitions, securities regulation, and compliance increasingly turn on what a company's technology actually does. Diligence goes faster when you can read the product itself.
